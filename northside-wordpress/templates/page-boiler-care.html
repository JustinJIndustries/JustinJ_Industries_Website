A boiler-care conversation should help you understand your system, plan maintenance and make sense of the next recommendation.

Start with what you know.

A useful visit starts with useful context. These details help frame the conversation.

Your equipment

The boiler’s make and model, approximate age if known, and any available service record.

What has changed

Heating that feels different, hot water that is less consistent, or a fault code already shown on the display.

What you want to understand

Ask what the visit covers, how findings will be explained and what any follow-up work would involve.

Maintenance, without the guesswork.

Use the manufacturer’s instructions and qualified advice for your particular equipment. This page is not a troubleshooting guide.

How often should a boiler be serviced?

Check the manufacturer’s guidance for your model. A qualified professional can advise on a suitable maintenance schedule.

Should I repair or replace my boiler?

Start with an assessment of the fault, the equipment’s condition and your heating needs. Compare the scope and cost of each option before deciding.

What should I have ready for an enquiry?

The make and model, a brief description of what has changed, any displayed fault code and the date of the last service are useful starting points. Do not open the casing or attempt a repair to gather this information.

Can I carry out the service myself?

Use an appropriately qualified professional for servicing and repairs. Do not open equipment, bypass controls or attempt a repair based on website content.
